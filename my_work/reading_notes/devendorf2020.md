# Making Design Memoirs: Understanding and Honoring Difficult Experiences

## 1 · Why this paper

- **Direct lineage to my weaving + RtD work:** Lead author Laura Devendorf bridges computational textiles and feminist STS—here, her *Exoskeleton for Sedimentation* (handwoven stiff cotton, stainless steel thread, and 13 force sensors) treats cloth and data not as optimization tools, but as surfaces for registering bodily wear, repetition, and care.
- **Anti-solutionist framing:** It models how to argue for design artifacts that *stay with* contradiction, friction, and unresolvable experience rather than "fixing" a problem—directly parallel to how *Polyrhythm Loom* resists flattening Cotai mudflat ecologies into clean, utilitarian dashboards.
- **Operationalized Baradian method:** It turns Karen Barad's *diffractive reading* into a concrete, repeatable design-research protocol for reading distinct material artifacts through one another.

## 2 · Summary

- **What it talks about:** The authors introduce *Design Memoirs*—a first-person, embodied design research method for expressing and honoring difficult, taboo, or unresolved lived experiences (specifically parenting, assisted fertility,and caregiving) through speculative, non-utilitarian wearable artifacts.
- **Problem & importance:** HCI overwhelmingly treats struggle and pain as "pain points" to resolve or optimize away, leaving designers with few methods or vocabularies to witness, commemorate, or reflect on how personal,corporeal experiences shape design practice.
- **Main contributions & innovation:**
  - Three interwoven Design Memoir series: *Intimate Exoskeletons* (Devendorf: crocheted/handwoven protective garments), *Done Medals* (Kelliher: 3D-printed medals commemorating fertility treatment cycles), and *Pockets* (Andersen: altered garments for secrecy, survival, and cross-generational grief).
  - A **diffractive reading** protocol: pairing the three projects into dyads (*Medals + Pockets*, *Pockets + Exoskeletons*, *Exoskeletons + Medals*) to generate *resonances* (*Marking Emotional Space*, *Staying with Contradiction*, *Staying with Struggle*).
  - Ethical/relational scaffolding for intimate collaboration, including an explicit *"drop-out contract"* and a shift from *empathy* (which can reinforce otherness) to *companionship*.
- **Advance over prior work:** Unlike autobiographical design and autoethnography—which center on designing a system, *using* it, and reporting on use—Design Memoirs abandon practical utility (*"trading utility for hyperbole"*) so the *making* process itself becomes a literary-material space for selective memory, emotional truth, and public "imprinting."

## 3 · Critical thinking

- **The "non-use" vs. "hypothetical use" contradiction:** In positioning Design Memoirs against autobiographical design (p. 4), the authors insist that utility and practicality must *"give way"* so makers can take narrative liberties.Yet when they shift to diffractive reading (p. 6), they explicitly reintroduce *"imagined or hypothetical use"* (*"how one might be used in tandem with the other"*) as the primary engine that makes dyads generative and emotionally safe (*"no one cried while writing the diffractional dyads"*). They never reconcile why *non-use* is required for making while *hypothetical use* is required for reading.
- **Unexamined asymmetry in making and delegation:** The paper frames making as intimate, first-person *"mattering,"* yet the artifacts span wildly different technical and labor registers—from Andersen hand-stitching a moth-eaten cashmere sweater to Kelliher's Tinkercad/3D-printed medallions and Devendorf's 13-sensor handwoven garment (which *Acknowledgments* notes involved prototyping/fabrication help from four collaborators and Virginia Tech's Dreams Lab). How external technical labor mediates what counts as "personal" or "secret" goes unexamined.
- **Theorized audience without observed reception:** The authors frame Design Memoirs as *"a project of communication"* meant to *"connect with a reader"* and *"imprint"* on HCI (pp. 3, 10), but the paper stops at the three authors reading *each other*. Whether an outside audience without the 3-page written backstory reads the artifacts as poignant, cryptic, or accidentally solutionist remains untested.

## 4 · Creative thinking

- **Diffractive reading across *Polyrhythm Loom*'s temporalities:** Instead of only comparing your shared environmental timeline against agent-specific generative rhythms analytically, you could run a *diffractive dyad* exercise—reading two woven/ecological traces *through* each other (e.g., tidal sedimentation vs. a mudskipper's rhythm) to articulate what "interference patterns" of difference your computational grammar produces.
- **Material wear alongside rule-based data:** *Exoskeleton for Sedimentation* pairs 13 force sensors with physical wear/dirt on denim-like cloth. In *Polyrhythm Loom*, this sparks an explicit reflection on how physical warp/weft tension, dye, or material accumulation in an exhibition space registers ecological friction differently from the computational rules running behind it.
- **Honoring rather than resolving ecological friction:** You can borrow their *"commemoration over delegation"* stance to frame why *Polyrhythm Loom* translates Cotai's shifting mudflat rhythms into tactile, ambiguous textile structures rather than "monitoring" or "solving" coastal change.

## 5 · Form — read it as a genre, not just as content

- **Length & proportions:** 12 pages total in CHI two-column format; ~9.5 pages of main text (pp. 1–10) and 58 references (pp. 10–12). Roughly 2.5 pages go to intro/theory (*What is a Design Memoir?*, *Personal in Public*, *Engagements*), ~1.5 pages to the three 1st-person artifact vignettes, ~2 pages to *Diffractively Reading* + *Resonances*, and ~2.5 pages to *Discussion* + *Conclusions*.
- **First contribution claim:** Appears in the Abstract and Introduction (p. 1): *"Our work aims to make space for alternative stories on the practice of design through first person and embodied design reflections that we call Design Memoirs."*
- **Artifact vs. analysis balance:** Lean artifact descriptions (~1.5 pages) paired with heavy theoretical framing and post-hoc diffractive/methodological analysis (~7 pages). The objects do their work through 8 evocative, studio-shot photographs and poetic captions rather than technical schematics.
- **Related work style:** Woven seamlessly into three short thematic subsections (pp. 2–4) rather than a separate "Related Work" dump—blending literary memoirists (Mary Karr, Maggie Nelson, Siri Hustvedt), feminist STS (Haraway, Barad,Ribbens), and RtD/first-person HCI (Gaver, Blythe, Höök, Desjardins & Ball, Rosner).
- **Process evidence & figures:** 8 figures total, all artifact photos plus one Tinkercad 3D render (Fig. 8); zero diagrams,charts, or user-study tables. Captions follow a crisp, recurring metadata template (*Materials / Method / Subjects*) followed by a 1st-person poetic reflection.
- **Voice, tense & limitations:** Shifts deliberately from collective *"we"* (theory, dyads, discussion) to individual *"I"* in the three memoir vignettes and captions. No standalone "Limitations" section—instead, they address positionality/privilege (p. 4), power dynamics and community risk (pp. 3, 9), and their *"informal drop-out contract"* (p. 9) inside the narrative.

## 6 · Venue signal

- **CHI vs. DIS:** Winning a CHI Honorable Mention with zero user studies, zero metrics, and 100% first-person speculative objects shows that even CHI rewards deeply personal RtD *when* it is packaged as a named, transferable **method** (*Design Memoirs* + *diffractive reading*) grounded in strong STS/HCI lineage.
- **What this means for DIS:** **DIS** is an even more natural home for this register. DIS reviewers won't expect you to disguise *Polyrhythm Loom* as an evaluation study—as long as your interpretive moves (how ecological processes become computational weaving rules) and your reflection on limitations are as rigorously named and structured as Devendorf et al.'s diffractive protocol.

## 7 · Take-home

- **What's new:** A remarkably clean way to operationalize Barad's *diffraction*—put two makers' artifacts side by side and ask what happens if you imagine them working together, using *difference* rather than shared sentiment to spark new insights. Also, the sharp conceptual pivot from *empathy* to *companionship*.
- **Application to *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Use their *non-use / anti-solutionist* framing in your Discussion to justify why your informal exhibition encounters and weaving grammar aim for *imprinting* and *staying with* more-than-human temporal friction rather than utilitarian accuracy.
- **Good writing sample to imitate?** **Yes, with one caveat.** Imitate its tight structural arc (Theory → Brief 1st-person Material Vignettes → Diffractive Reading → Resonances → Methodological Discussion) and its *Materials / Method / Subjects* caption format for your textile artifacts. Unlike this paper, however, your inclusion of informal exhibition encounters gives you empirical grounding with external viewers—keep that, as it directly answers this paper's biggest blind spot.
