# Exploring the Pluralities of More-than-Human Biographies through Speculative Maps

## 1 · Why this paper

Published at **DIS ’26** by Ron Wakkary’s Everyday Design Studio, this pictorial is a direct benchmark for my target [venue.It](http://venue.It) tackles a core challenge shared by *Polyrhythm Loom*—how to represent the plurality of more-than-human lives and temporalities—and arrives at insights around **"materials as time"** and **"forming of assemblages,"** making it essential to compare against my computational weaving approach.

## 2 · Summary

What it talks about: A DIS ’26 pictorial experimenting with **speculative mapping** to trace the multiple more-than-human "lives" or biographies of a previously designed research artifact, *wi-fi-no-wi-fi*.

- Main contributions: Presents **three speculative maps** generated through workshops, empirical investigations, and speculation, yielding design insights into (1) materials as time, (2) forming of assemblages and spaces, and (3) multispecies perspectives of regeneration.
- Problem & importance: Even within more-than-human design (MTHD), it is hard to grasp the *plurality* of how designed things actually live, degrade, and entangle with nonhuman worlds beyond a single human-use narrative.
- Innovation & benefits: Uses speculative mapping as a generative, visual RtD method rather than a purely analytical cartography, letting designers trace multiple, simultaneous biographies of the same artifact.
- Advance over prior work: Extends Wakkary’s earlier theoretical work on *more-than-human biographies* (2024) and *things*(*Things We Could Design*) into a concrete **visual/pictorial mapping practice** at DIS.



## 3 · Critical thinking

- Cartographic freezing of live temporalities: Even speculative maps tend to flatten dynamic, ongoing processes onto a 2D visual plane for human inspection. Calling them *"materials as time"* highlights a tension: a static map can *diagram* time retrospectively, but it cannot *unfold, accumulate, or interrupt* in real time.
- Object-centric gravity: Tracing the plural biographies of a single studio-built device (*wi-fi-no-wi-fi*) still keeps a **human-designed artifact** at the center of the story, with multispecies worlds orbiting around it—whereas site-situated ecological systems (like a mudflat) have rhythms that precede and exceed any single device.
- Blurred boundary between empirical trace and studio imagination: Combining workshops, empirical investigations, and speculation into three polished maps risks masking which claims came from actual material/ecological observation versus human-led workshop projection.



## 4 · Creative thinking

- Sparks: Their insight on **"materials as time"** pairs naturally with *Polyrhythm Loom*. Where Wakkary et al. use *maps* to trace how materials move through time, I use *digital weaving* to let different temporalities literally construct and deform a shared material structure.
- New application: *Polyrhythm Loom*’s accumulated digital fabric could itself be read (or annotated) as a **live speculative map** of Cotai mudflat biographies—where each strand records a species, tide, model, or visitor encounter.
- Alternative method: Instead of assembling retrospective 2D maps after workshops, *Polyrhythm Loom* makes **relational composition generative and conditional in real time**, allowing temporal asymmetries to become structurally visible as connections form or break.



## 5 · Form — read it as a genre, not just as content

- Length & format: **14 pages** (pp. 3188–3201) in the **DIS ’26 Pictorial** track.
- First contribution claim (verbatim from Abstract):
  > *"This pictorial offers three speculative maps and revealed design insights into materials as time, forming of assemblages and spaces, and multispecies perspectives of regeneration."*
- Artifact vs. analysis: In DIS Pictorials, **visual artifacts (the three maps) carry the primary argumentative weight**, paired with concise reflective synthesis rather than traditional text-heavy analysis.
- Related work & process evidence: Anchored in *more-than-human biographies* and *speculative design*, evidenced through **workshop outputs, empirical investigations, and visual map spreads** rather than statistical user studies.
- Voice & tense: First-person plural (*"we experiment", "we speculated"*); past tense for the mapping process and findings, present tense for what the pictorial offers.



## 6 · Venue signal

This is a crystal-clear signal from **DIS ’26** itself: DIS actively rewards **more-than-human RtD/speculative work** that produces *visual/material ways of knowing* (here, speculative maps) and distills **conceptual design insights** (*materials as time, assemblages, regeneration*)—without requiring controlled user experiments or psychometric metrics. If *Polyrhythm Loom* is submitted to DIS (either as a Full Paper or Pictorial), pairing rich visual documentation of the unfolding fabric with your three formal operations (*connection, mediation, relational composition*) hits the exact sweet spot DIS values.

## 7 · Take-home

- Learned & new: Framing a design case around **"pluralities of biographies"** is a great way to avoid telling a single, tidy story about an ecological site or prototype.
- Application: Cite this DIS ’26 paper in *Polyrhythm Loom* to position **computational weaving** alongside **speculative mapping** as complementary ways of representing more-than-human temporalities—highlighting how weaving adds *real-time structural accumulation and interruption* where maps remain static.
- Good writing sample? Because it’s a **Pictorial**, it’s a great model if you ever consider a DIS Pictorial format (visual spreads doing the heavy lifting), though for a Full Paper you’ll want more explicit detail on your computational grammar and exhibition reflections.
