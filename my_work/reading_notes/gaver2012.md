# What Should We Expect from Research Through Design?

## 1 · Why this paper

- **Methodological shield for *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Gaver (2012) is the foundational CHI/DIS argument for why Research through Design (RtD) produces *provisional, contingent, and aspirational* theory rather than falsifiable, generalizable laws—directly backing your decision to evaluate *Polyrhythm Loom* through reflective making and informal exhibition encounters rather than controlled usability metrics.
- **Annotated Portfolios:** His proposal to treat theory as *annotation* across a collection of realized artifacts offers a ready-made visual and structural strategy for presenting multiple *Polyrhythm Loom* swatches/experiments together without erasing their material particularity.

## 2 · Summary

- **What it talks about:** A single-author meta-theoretical essay pushing back against calls to standardize RtD into a formal, "scientific" methodology within HCI.
- **Problem & importance:** As RtD gained traction at CHI, prominent voices (e.g., Zimmerman, Stolterman, Forlizzi) called for agreed-upon standards, protocols, and "extensible and verifiable" theory. Gaver warns that importing tacit "scientism" into design risks policing away its greatest strength: creative, generative speculation.
- **Main contributions & innovation:**
  - **Philosophy of Science foil:** Contrasts Popperian *falsifiability* with Lakatos's *scientific research programmes* and Kuhn's *paradigms* to show that design theory is inherently unfalsifiable because *theory underspecifies design* and *design is generative* (concerned with *what might be*, not *what is*).
  - **Three explanations for RtD's lack of convergence:** (1) it's *pre-paradigmatic*; (2) consensus is *invisible* because papers debate controversies rather than shared assumptions; or (3) design inhabits **Many Worlds** (borrowing Law & Urry's *ontological politics*), where multiple incompatible worlds legitimately coexist.
  - **Theory as Annotation / Annotated Portfolios:** Proposes *annotated portfolios* (developed with John Bowers) as RtD's native theoretical form—juxtaposing realized design examples with concise conceptual annotations to map a design space without abstracting away the *"ultimate particular."*
- **Advance over prior work:** Reframed the CHI/DIS RtD debate from *"how do we make design look like science?"* to *"what kind of knowledge does design actually produce on its own terms?"*

## 3 · Critical thinking

- **The "extensibility and verifiability" contradiction:** Gaver spends two-thirds of the essay arguing that RtD theory cannot and should not be extensible and verifiable. Yet at the end of *Annotated Portfolios*, he claims annotated portfolios respect the ultimate particular *"while allowing for the 'extensibility and verifiability' for which some of the HCI design community have called"*—without ever explaining how a visual-conceptual collage of Dieter Rams appliances actually achieves "verifiability."
- **Treating artifacts as pre-theoretical "facts":** Declaring *"it is the artefacts we create that are the definite facts of research through design"* and theory as mere annotation sets up a neat hierarchy (artifact = primary truth; theory = post-hoc caption). That overlooks how theoretical commitments and computational assumptions already structure making from the start—especially in algorithmic/data-driven RtD like *Polyrhythm Loom*, where the weaving grammar *is* a materialized theory.
- **Unexamined gatekeeping behind "artefacts speak":** Rejecting formal evaluation standards as *"potentially repressive acts of ontological politics"* protects creative diversity, but Gaver never asks *who* decides what counts as a "conceptually rich artefact" when shared criteria are absent. Leaning on tacit connoisseurship (exemplified by canonizing Dieter Rams) risks trading explicit methodological gatekeeping for an informal, clubby taste-based gatekeeping.

## 4 · Creative thinking

- **An Annotated Portfolio figure for *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Instead of treating your woven pieces as isolated illustrations, build one key figure as a Gaver-style **annotated portfolio** of 3–4 swatches/runs (e.g., shared environmental timeline vs. agent-specific generative rhythms vs. hybrid interference), using concise callout notes to map your design space (*connection, mediation, relational composition*) and surface where translation broke down.
- **Framing Cotai through *ontological politics*:** Gaver's borrowing of Law & Urry—that generative practices make *"multiple worlds... equally valid, equally true, but simply unlike one another"*—gives you sharp language to position *Polyrhythm Loom* alongside coastal ecology: not competing to model Cotai "accurately," but enacting one situated, material-temporal world.

## 6 · Venue signal

- **CHI '12 vs. DIS:** Published at **CHI '12** (where reviewer R4 noted it addressed *"an elephant in the room...: the move towards 'scientism' in HCI"*), this paper became **DIS's** unofficial constitution.
- **What this means for DIS:** At DIS, citing Gaver (2012) to justify why your contribution lies in *situated making, relational annotation, and conceptual fertility* rather than hypothesis testing is standard, welcomed practice—especially when paired with honest reflection on interpretive choices and limits.

## 7 · Take-home

- **What's new / enduring:** (1) Why RtD theory is *provisional, contingent, and aspirational*; (2) *Many Worlds* / ontological politics as a defense against forced convergence; and (3) **Annotated Portfolios** as a rigorous middle ground between pure particularism and over-abstracted rules.
- **Application to *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Cite Gaver (2012) in your Methodology to anchor your RtD approach and exhibition encounters, and consider structuring your swatch/artifact showcase as an **annotated portfolio**.
- **Good writing sample to imitate?** **Yes—for clarity, wit, and argumentative structure.** Notice how Gaver uses crisp, memorable subheadings (*Theory Underspecifies Design*, *Design Is Generative*, *The Invisibility of Consensus*) and concrete foils to make dense philosophy of science effortless to read.
