# Caring through: Engaging with temporality of care in more-than-human design

## 1 · Why this paper

- **Shared temporal mechanics:** Builds directly on Pschetz & Bastian (2018) to foreground **synchrony**, **asynchrony**,and **latency**—the exact temporal dynamics *Polyrhythm Loom* stages when contrasting a shared environmental timeline with agent-specific generative rhythms.
- **Mediated relationality:** Frames *caring through* as a *mediated*, cascading relationship where roles between actors blur across time, resonating strongly with your weaving-inspired computational grammar (*connection, mediation, relational composition*) in Cotai's mudflat.

## 2 · Summary

- **What it talks about:** A conceptual paper that bridges more-than-human (MtH) care ethics and temporality research in HCI by proposing a four-part typology of care—*caring about, for, with,* and **caring through**.
- **Problem & importance:** Care risks becoming an unexamined buzzword in MtH design; existing English prepositions (*caring for* or *about*) imply one-way, human-centered, instantaneous relationships that miss how MtH care unfolds across plural, misaligned, and long-delayed timescales.
- **Main contributions & innovation:**
  - A **typology of care** organized around time, labor, and agency (*about*: moral stance; *for*: practical labor; *with*: shared infrastructures/systems; *through*: temporally flexible, cascading mediation).
  - The concept of **caring through** (and *"ripples of care"*): caring for one entity (e.g., growing mycelium for soil mycoremediation, or reintroducing wolves in Yellowstone) cascades into care for other human and non-human beings across **synchrony**, **asynchrony**, and **latency**.
  - Three design implications: accessing MtH temporalities through durational care, designing for cascading *"ripples of care,"* and treating AI as an interdependent *intermediate* partner.
- **Advance over prior work:** Connects two HCI strands that usually run separately—*MtH care* (Bellacasa, Zhou et al., Key et al.) and *temporality/temporal design* (Pschetz & Bastian, Odom et al.)—into a unified vocabulary.

## 3 · Critical thinking

- **Conflating trophic predation and management with "care":** Their flagship ecological examples—goats eating wildfire brush and Yellowstone wolves preying on deer so riparian plants recover (p. 4)—stretch feminist care ethics to cover predation and land management without addressing the **violence or instrumentalization** inside those cascades. Calling every indirect ecological chain *"ripples of care"* risks romanticizing multispecies entanglement and hiding who pays the cost (the deer, or sterile lab-grown fungi deployed into toxic waste).
- **Resolving temporal frustration by "leaving the stage":** In the mycoremediation vignette (Section 4.0.1), the first author admits *"daily visits to the site can become a source of disappointment, as mycelium operates on a different timescale and pace than the anticipated minutes, hours, and days of human labor."* Yet the Discussion resolves this only rhetorically—urging humans to *"quietly leave the stage"* (p. 6)—without explaining how design researchers or interactive systems actually sustain attention, maintenance, or accountability across multi-year latencies.
- **Uncritical leap from living organisms to AI (Section 5.3):** Jumping in half a column from soil and fungi to *"Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a more-than-human entity"* treats AI as an *"interdependent partner in care practices"* without addressing AI's extractive energy/material footprint or its lack of biological vulnerability and reciprocity (which Section 3.1 grounded in growth rates, soil pH, and wagging tails).

## 4 · Creative thinking

- **Naming temporal relations in *Polyrhythm Loom*'s weave grammar:** Use Oktay et al.'s crisp triad—**synchrony**(aligned rhythms), **asynchrony** (divergent/interfering rhythms), and **latency** (delayed effects)—to articulate what your weaving grammar actually *computes* when Cotai's environmental timeline meets agent-specific rhythms.
- **"Mediation" as *caring through*:** In your Related Work or Discussion, connect your grammar term *mediation* to *caring through*: one mudflat agent's rhythm (e.g., tidal inundation) acts *through* the textile structure to reshape another agent's pattern, turning the cloth into a record of cascading temporal interdependence rather than isolated data streams.

## 5 · Form — read it as a genre, not just as content

- **Length & proportions:** 8 pages total (pp. 1–8); **~5.5 pages excluding references** (61 references on pp. 6–8). Body breakdown: Abstract + Intro ~0.8 p.; Related Work ~1.5 pp.; Background (Typologies) ~0.8 p.; *Caring through* + Table + Fungi vignette ~1.4 pp.; Discussion + Positionality + Conclusion ~1.1 pp.
- **First contribution claim & verbatim sentence:** Appears in the Introduction (p. 1): *"Specifically, we provide the notion caring through as a generative term to highlight the role of time in care and the fluctuating care roles that are not fully captured via existing terms."*
- **Artifact vs. analysis balance:** Almost entirely a **conceptual/theoretical essay** (~90% literature synthesis and typology),supported by three secondary ecological examples and one brief (~0.4-page) first-author mycoremediation vignette (Section 4.0.1).
- **Related work style:** Organized into two clean thematic streams (*2.1 Care as a lens in HCI and design research* with *2.1.1 Care in MtH design*, and *2.2 Time and temporality in HCI and design research*), explicitly setting up the gap where the two streams haven't yet met.
- **Figures & tables:** **1 figure** (Fig. 1: 3-panel photo of mycelium on soil at placement, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks with worms) and **1 unnumbered summary table** mapping *About / For / With / Through* to associations and examples.
- **Voice, tense & limitations:** Collective *"we"* throughout, shifting briefly to third-person *"the first author (FA)"* / *"she"* in Section 4.0.1. Present tense for conceptual claims; past tense for the mycoremediation practice and conclusion. No standalone "Limitations" section—instead, **Section 5.4 (*Positionality statement*)** reflects on how English prepositions (*about, for, with, through*) enforce a carer/cared-for binary that may not translate across languages.

## 6 · Venue signal

- **HTTF vs. DIS:** *Halfway to the Future (HTTF)* is an intimate, speculative/provocation-friendly ACM symposium where an 8-page conceptual essay with a single brief practice vignette thrives.
- **What this means for DIS:** At **DIS**, a 5.5-page conceptual typology without a full RtD artifact/study would likely feel under-evidenced—which works in *Polyrhythm Loom*'s favor, because you already have the computational weaving system, Cotai fieldwork, and exhibition encounters to ground these temporal concepts materially.

## 7 · Take-home

- **What's new:** The four-part preposition typology (*about / for / with / through*) and the concrete temporal vocabulary of **synchrony, asynchrony, latency,** and **"ripples of care."**
- **Application to *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Cite Oktay et al. (2024) in your temporal framing to connect *more-than-human temporalities* with *mediation*, and consider adapting their *Positionality Statement* move (reflecting on how your English/computational terms shape what ecological relations become legible).
- **Good writing sample to imitate?** **Yes for conceptual synthesis and Section 5.4's positionality framing**; just don't imitate its ultra-light artifact evidence for a full DIS submission.
