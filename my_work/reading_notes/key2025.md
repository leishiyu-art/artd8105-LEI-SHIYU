# Leaky Cups: Tinkering with Hydrofeminist Temporalities for HCI

## 1 · Why this paper

- **Hydrofeminist & more-than-human temporalities:** Directly aligns with *Polyrhythm Loom*'s focus on *more-than-human temporalities* in a coastal wetland (Cotai mudflat), framing ecological time as *"hydrologic rather than technologic"*.
- **Materializing multiple timescales in one artifact:** Just as *Polyrhythm Loom* contrasts a shared environmental timeline with agent-specific rhythms, *Leaky Cups* physically juxtaposes two watershed timescales (30 years ago vs. real-time) inside a single domestic object.

## 2 · Summary

- **What it talks about:** A Research-through-Design inquiry that asks what home IoT might look like if it followed *hydrocycles* rather than *productionist cycles*—materialized through Wi-Fi-connected, porous terracotta cups linked to Washington State USGS watershed data.
- **Problem & importance:** Everyday connected devices enforce anthropocentric, productionist rhythms that obscure our bodily and ecological entanglement with planetary water systems and non-human timescales.
- **Main contributions & innovation:**
  - **The Leaky Cups artifacts:** Porous terracotta cups with walnut bases housing a microcontroller, motor, battery, and Wi-Fi. Tiny holes hold water via surface tension until incoming USGS stream-discharge data vibrates the cup, creating wave patterns that break surface tension and make the cup leak.
  - **Dual-timescale actuation:** Each cup vibrates *twice* per update—first for the watershed's discharge rate at that exact date/time **30 years ago**, then for the **real-time** discharge rate—turning abstract hydrological history into a palpable, messy domestic event.
  - **Theoretical synthesis:** Brings Astrida Neimanis's *hydrofeminism* (*"Hypersea"*), Maria Puig de la Bellacasa's care temporalities, and Barbara Adam's *timescapes* into physical computing, framing the home as an *"ecotone"* between human bodies and bodies of water.
- **Advance over prior work:** Moves posthumanist/hydrofeminist temporality beyond purely conceptual critique into a working, data-driven physical-computing probe that uses material physics (surface tension) rather than screen displays.

## 3 · Critical thinking

- **Collapsing a watershed into one scalar metric:** While the framing evokes rich multispecies hydro-ecologies (*"book lice, swamp cabbage, freshwater mussels"*, rain clouds, sedimentation tanks), the computational mechanism reduces an entire watershed to a single USGS stream-gauge number (*discharge rate*)—quietly re-centering a standardized, state-instrumented measurement.
- **Two discrete pulses vs. continuous ecological rhythms:** Buzzing once for 30 years ago and once for now sets up a neat *then-vs-now* comparison, but flattens the ongoing, asynchronous, and multi-agent temporalities of a watershed (e.g., groundwater lag, tidal/seasonal shifts, dam regulation) into two isolated triggers.
- **Unexamined domestic care burden of "leaking":** Making a cup deliberately leak on a household surface playfully disrupts smooth IoT utility, yet in domestic space, wiping up water falls on everyday care labor—raising a tension (not addressed on the project page) between *symbolic* care for watersheds and *literal* maintenance work at home.

## 4 · Creative thinking

- **Deep-time / baseline juxtaposition in *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Borrow their 30-year-ago vs. real-time pairing—for instance, contrasting Cotai mudflat's *historical* tidal/reclamation baseline against *current* rhythms within your woven structure, rather than only representing present-day agents.
- **Physical thresholds doing computational work:** *Leaky Cups* doesn't use a mechanical valve; it lets *surface tension + vibration* decide when water escapes. In *Polyrhythm Loom*, this sparks thinking about where physical textile properties (warp tension, yarn twist, moisture absorption) act as material thresholds rather than passive outputs of code.
- **Framing the loom/exhibition as an *ecotone*:** Neimanis's concept of an *ecotone* (a liminal transition zone between ecological communities) is a sharp metaphor for *Polyrhythm Loom*'s exhibition encounters—a meeting ground between human visitors, computational rules, and Cotai's coastal mudflat.

## 6 · Venue signal

- **CHI '25 acceptance of feminist-STS RtD:** A 17-page paper centered on *"tinkering with hydrofeminist temporalities"* via speculative, intentionally "malfunctioning" (leaky) IoT cups getting into CHI's main track shows how mainstream HCI has opened up to post-solutionist, more-than-human temporality work.
- **What this means for DIS:** **DIS** is an even more native venue for this style of work (as seen in Key et al.'s earlier DIS '22 paper *Feminist Care in the Anthropocene*), especially when your computational weaving grammar is backed by concrete physical artifacts and honest reflection on ecological translation.

## 7 · Take-home

- **What's new:** Operationalizing *hydrofeminism* and *timescapes* through material physics (ceramic porosity + surface tension) and dual-timestamped environmental data.
- **Application to *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Cite Key et al. (2025) alongside Pschetz & Bastian (2018) to ground your contrast between *productionist/technologic time* and *more-than-human/hydrologic temporalities* in a coastal wetland context.
- **Good writing sample to imitate?** From the project text, their opening *"What if..."* questions and use of *"ecotone"* are super evocative and clear—definitely worth modeling in your Introduction/Framing, though you'll want institutional access to the full 17-page PDF to study how they structure their findings and limitations for CHI.
