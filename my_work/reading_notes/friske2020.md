# Entangling the Roles of Maker and Interpreter in Interpersonal Data Narratives: Explorations in Yarn and Sound

## 1 · Why this paper

- **Data as relation & material negotiation:** Directly models *Polyrhythm Loom*'s core premise—treating data not as an objective truth to be decoded, but as one material among others (*yarn, sound, time, language*) whose form and meaning are co-authored through making and interpretation.
- **Explicit reflection on translation compromises:** Shows *how* to write honestly about forcing messy lived phenomena into structural/material rules (e.g., Mikhaila regrouping commute categories to hit a 2:1 triangle-to-square crochet ratio),which mirrors your reflection on translating Cotai mudflat processes into weaving grammar.
- **DIS benchmark:** A **DIS '20 Best Paper Honorable Mention** from Devendorf's lab that turns a small, two-person autobiographical exchange into a rigorous methodological contribution.

## 2 · Summary

- **What it talks about:** Over several months, the first two authors (Mikhaila and Jordan) each collected one week of personal data on three shared topics—**commuting**, **forgetting**, and **busy-ness**—crafted them by hand into **yarn**(crochet/knit) and **sound** (audio compositions/interfaces), and exchanged the artifacts *without keys or legends* to see how narratives emerge and diverge between maker and interpreter.
- **Problem & importance:** Personal informatics and data visualization privilege screen-based numeric precision,individual optimization, and "authoritative" data doubles, hiding the subjective labor of data collection and treating ambiguity as a failure.
- **Main contributions & innovation:**
  - Six paired artifacts across yarn and sound (crochet commuting patches, a knitted forgetting scarf with "bulbs" and holes, a busy-ness mug cozy with a tangled interior, a 2-minute commuting soundscape, a randomized 30-clip *Sounds of Forgetting* web interface, and an Alexa busy-ness audio drama).
  - Four empirical themes: (1) *matching data to materials (and vice versa)*; (2) *accepting the materials' will to co-author* (including *time* as a material); (3) *contrasting the data of the experience vs. the experience of the data* (including representing uncollectable data like "forgetting" through absence/holes); and (4) *the co-existence of multiple narratives*.
  - The **maker-interpreter** concept (adapting Barthes' *Death of the Author*): rather than the maker dying for the interpreter to be born, both roles vacillate and are continually "reborn" into each other, distributing narrative authority across humans and materials.
- **Advance over prior work:** Extends *Dear Data* (Lupi & Posavec) from 2D hand-drawn postcards with detailed legends between strangers to multi-sensory physicalization/sonification *without* legends between colleagues and friends—exposing what happens when interpretive scaffolding is deliberately withheld.

## 3 · Critical thinking

- **Unexamined third-author asymmetry:** Even though Laura Devendorf is third author and the paper uses collective *"we"* throughout, the entire autobiographical pipeline—data collection, making, 5–10 minute voice reactions, and 30-minute debriefs—occurs strictly between *"Mikhaila and Jordan"* (pp. 297, 299–301). Devendorf's analytical or framing role in the thematic analysis (p. 301) is never articulated, leaving the "entanglement" dyadic in practice while triadic on the byline.
- **The Barthesian "rebirth" masks asymmetric reveal power:** Claiming *"no narrative invalidates another"* (p.306) sounds egalitarian, yet their own accounts show uneven interpretive leverage: Jordan notes her *"lack of knowledge about knitting"* forced her to lean on personal memories of forgetting (p. 305), while Mikhaila's familiarity with Jordan let them guess podcast clips (Fig. 2). Moreover, because they reconvened to explain *"what we had actually done"* (p. 301) and structured every caption as `non-maker` vs. `maker`, the maker's original intent still sits as the hidden anchor against which the interpreter's reading is compared.
- **When "listening to the material" becomes data gerrymandering:** To achieve a 2:1 triangle-to-square crochet ratio, Mikhaila abandoned a *bus vs. car* split (which yielded 3:1) and regrouped commutes into *"typical vs. atypical"* (p. 302). Framing this solely as productive material-data negotiation glosses over a sharper ethical boundary: when translating *external* or *ecological* data (rather than your own commute), altering categories to please a weave geometry risks severing accountability to the phenomenon itself.

## 4 · Creative thinking

- **Dual-voice captions for *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Borrow their brilliant `maker` vs. `non-maker` caption pairing (Figs. 1–6) to document your informal exhibition encounters—putting *your* computational weaving rule/intent side-by-side with an *exhibition visitor's* unguided reading of the same woven section.
- **Treating *time* and *missingness* as explicit weaving materials:** Just as Mikhaila used physical **holes** in the scarf to represent *"un-noted forgettances"* without fabricating data (p. 304), *Polyrhythm Loom* can use structural voids, skipped picks, or unpatterned warp floats to mark moments where Cotai mudflat sensors or ecological rhythms slipped outside your computational grammar.
- **Naming *apophenia* as an exhibition dynamic:** Their Discussion note on *apophenia* (p. 306)—that simply telling someone *"data was involved"* makes them strain to find connections—is a sharp lens for analyzing how exhibition visitors project ecological meaning onto algorithmic textile patterns.

## 5 · Form — read it as a genre, not just as content

- **Length & proportions:** 14 pages total (pp. 297–310); **10 pages excluding references** (76 references on pp. 307–310).Allocation across the 10 body pages: Abstract/Intro ~1.25 pp.; Related Work ~1.8 pp.; Methods ~1.7 pp.; The Artefacts ~1.0 p.; Findings ~4.0 pp.; Discussion/Conclusion ~1.5 pp.
- **First contribution claim & verbatim sentence:** Appears in the Introduction (p. 297): *"We present the six artefacts created by Mikhaila and Jordan as well as themes that emerged from reflecting on the process of making and exchanging these data narratives: (1) matching data to the material (and vice versa); (2) accepting the materials' will to co-author; (3) contrasting the experience of the data with data of the experience; and (4) embracing the co-existence of multiple narratives."* (Reinforced in Conclusion, p. 306: *"We contribute our maker-interpreter concept to the DIS community and offer paths for future work."*)
- **Artifact vs. analysis balance:** Very tight artifact descriptions (~1 page of 1st-person making summaries) paired with deep, example-rich thematic analysis (~4 pages) and theory-driven discussion (~1.5 pages). The artifacts carry equal weight *through* the 6 large, annotated figures.
- **Related work style:** Structured into three crisp thematic mini-essays (*Brief History of Personal Informatics*, *The Authority of Data*, *From Visualization to Physicalization and Sonification*), each ending with a clear pivot into how this paper extends or departs from that strand.
- **Process evidence & figures:** 6 figures—3 textile photo collages with callout boxes (Figs. 1, 3, 5) and 3 bespoke visual diagrams/storyboards for the non-visual sound pieces (Fig. 2 multi-track timeline schematic; Fig. 4 web UI + layered sound-block diagram; Fig. 6 comic-strip dialogue of the Alexa audio drama). Zero tables or quantitative charts.
- **Voice, tense & limitations:** Collective *"we"* throughout, switching intentionally to 1st-person *"I"* in *The Artefacts* (p. 301) and the split figure captions. No standalone "Limitations" section; instead, they note preexisting friendship/trust as a boundary condition in *Methods* (p. 299) and flag stranger-exchanges and *apophenia* in *Implications and Future Work* (p.306).

## 6 · Venue signal

- **DIS sweet spot:** Winning a **DIS '20 Best Paper Honorable Mention** with a two-person RtD exchange, zero external user studies, and rich visual/schematic documentation of non-visual and textile artifacts shows that DIS strongly rewards **craft-inflected, reflective critiques of data authority**.
- **Takeaway for *Polyrhythm Loom*:** DIS reviewers love when you show *how* material constraints (yarn, time,software) pushed back against your original design plan—treating "failures" or compromises (like Mikhaila scaling down from a 24-patch pillow to 6 patches) as central methodological findings rather than hiding them.

## 7 · Take-home

- **What's new:** Operationalizing Barthes' *Death of the Author* into a circular **maker-interpreter** model where materials (*yarn, sound, time*) act as co-authors and *"no narrative invalidates another."*
- **Application to *Polyrhythm Loom*:** Use their split-caption format (`maker` vs. `visitor/interpreter` reading) for your exhibition figures, and cite their *apophenia* and *material-vs-data negotiation* themes in your Discussion when reflecting on the ethics and limits of translating mudflat ecology into computational weave structures.
- **Good writing sample to imitate?** **Yes—an A+ model for DIS.** Notice especially how they visualize non-static/temporal work (Figs. 2, 4, 6 use schematics, timelines, and comic panels so sound feels legible on paper)—a great trick if you need to show temporal *polyrhythms* alongside static cloth photos!
