## 2 · Summary

[AGENT DRAFT — rewrite this in your own words. A draft left in place does not count as your summary.]

- **What does this paper talk about?** Metamorphonic: three habitats for domestic silk moths, *Bombyx mori* (larva, pupa, adult). Movement in the habitats is sensed and turned into ambient music. The first author lived with the insects for about four months and reflects, through autoethnography, on the relationship the project actually produced.
- **What are its main contributions?** Three, on proceedings page 809. A case that ties cohabitation, habitat design, and a living artefact together. Habitability read through materiality and temporality. A reflection on the relations and ethical problems that showed up in the practice, including behind the exhibited object.
- **What problem does it address, and why is that problem important?** Sericulture keeps silkworms for silk and usually kills them at the pupal stage. The project asks whether a relationship can be “not about silk,” and what humans receive if they do not control the insects.
- **What is the innovation in the proposed solution, and what are its key benefits?** The habitats (Dome, Temple, Tower) are designed around the life cycle, and the music is offered as what the humans receive. The paper then sets that intention against the backstage: deadlines, growing numbers, care that slipped, and habitats tuned to look good to people.
- **How does it advance the state of the art — what does it do that previous work did not?** [AGENT DRAFT] Related work says HCI has few invertebrate cases, and little that uses materiality and temporality to understand more-than-human entanglements. This paper’s own claim of novelty is the combination above, plus the split between the public artefact and the process behind it.

## 3 · Critical thinking

The authors already concede, in §5.5 and §5.6: the habitats were not optimised from biological data; the insects also did fine in cardboard and egg crates; the effect of the music on them was not studied; the finish was for human attention and exhibition; human deadlines overrode the insects’ pace; care got worse as the population grew; and “not controlling them” was not fulfilled, because silk was replaced by the project, the exhibition, and the music. Your limitations have to go beyond those.

- **The contribution sentence, verbatim:** “The contribution of this paper to the ongoing more-than-human design discourse in design and HCI is threefold: (1) Unpacking a case that ties cohabitation with other beings, habitat design, and designing living artefacts; (2) Understanding/analysing habitability through the lenses of materiality and temporality; and (3) Reflecting on the emergent relations and ethical concerns throughout the design practice.” (p. 809)
- **Artifact description vs. analysis** — §4 (the habitats) and §5 (the backstage reflection) take about the same space. The paper sets that split up on purpose: the public face, then what was not presented.
- **What does related work do** — it groups human–animal HCI, habitat design, and materiality/temporality, and it uses the last of these to name a thin spot: few tangible cases that use those lenses for more-than-human entanglements.
- **How is process evidenced?** First-author autoethnography over about four months, the design iterations of three habitats, and the Dutch Design Week 2022 exhibition (Figure 10). Figure 11 shows the unofficial boxes the insects actually lived in.
- **Figures:** 11. Habitats, material tests, electrodes, the music setup, the exhibition, and the backstage containers. They carry both the designed object and the gap between that object and the keeping.
- **First person or passive? Present or past tense?** First person “we”, with “FA” for the first author in the autoethnography. The project is past tense. The claims are present.
- **Limitations — present? How much space?** Yes. §5.5 is a short subsection on the habitats. §5.6 then spends longer on the ways the project repeated the relation it meant to leave.
