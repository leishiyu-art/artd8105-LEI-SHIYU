# Metamorphonic: A Reflective Design Inquiry into Human-Silkworm Relationship

PDF supplied from the student's Zotero library on 2026-10-11. Front matter and section 5 were checked against that file. Proceedings pages run 808–819. The PDF text drops some fi/fl ligatures; those are restored in the quotation below. Sections 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 are still theirs.

## 1 · Why this paper
Published at DIS ’23 by Yuta Ikeya, Ron Wakkary, and Bahareh Barati, this paper is a prime example of how DIS frames more-than-human temporality through an interactive RtD artifact. Its two-part structure—pairing a material/temporal design journey with a brutally honest ethical reflection on care, control, and reward—offers a direct structural and reflexive model for Polyrhythm Loom.

## 2 · Summary

What it talks about: Explores an alternative, non-utilitarian relationship between humans and domesticated silkworms (Bombyx mori) by designing a triad of sensor-embedded habitats across their metamorphosis stages that turn silkworm movement into an improvised ambient synthesizer soundscape.
Main contributions: (1) Three interactive habitats attuned to the silkworm’s lifecycle and temporality; (2) a first-person RtD account framed through materiality and temporality; (3) a critical, autoethnographic reflection unpacking how care, control, and reward shift when rearing living organisms for an interactive exhibition.
Problem & importance: Silkworms have been selectively bred for over 5,000 years into flightless, dependent creatures for silk extraction, yet are simultaneously treated as sacred in Japanese sericulture—posing a sharp ethical tension for Animal-Computer Interaction (ACI) and more-than-human design.
Innovation & benefits: Instead of harvesting silk, the human caretaker receives an uncontrollable ambient soundscape generated by capacitive sensors tracking the insects' movements—contesting utilitarian extraction while making the species' own temporal rhythm audible.
Advance over prior work: Moves ACI/biodesign beyond animal-welfare optimization or novelty bio-interfaces by turning the designer’s own complicity and shifting relationship during pre-exhibition rearing into the core empirical subject.

## 4 · Creative thinking

Sparks: Metamorphonic’s pairing of materiality + temporality as analytical lenses, plus its focus on uncontrollable output in exchange for care, resonates strongly with Polyrhythm Loom’s dual temporal modes and visitor participation.
New application: Polyrhythm Loom could adopt a similar exhibition reflexivity—explicitly documenting how gallery constraints (visitor footfall, museum opening hours) clash with mudflat ecological rhythms (tides, seasonal migrations), making that friction part of the DIS discussion rather than hiding it.
Alternative method: Rather than working directly with live, captive organisms (which raises rearing and exhibition-ethics burdens), Polyrhythm Loom works with multi-source ecological records and models, shifting the ethical question from biological captivity to representational and computational mediation (connection, mediation, relational composition).

## 5 · Form — read it as a genre, not just as content

Length & format: 12 pages (pp. 808–819) in the DIS ’23 Full Papers track.
Genre & structure: Two-part Reflective RtD + Autoethnographic Inquiry—Intro & Related Work (ACI, sericulture history) 
→
→ Part 1: Design journey of habitats through materiality and temporality 
→
→ Part 2: Critical reflection on care, control, reward via autoethnography.
Artifact vs. analysis: Balanced—habitats and capacitive-sensing setup are grounded in lifecycle material needs, then paired with deep ethical/relational self-reflection.
Process evidence: First-person autoethnography, lifecycle/relationship diagrams (e.g., life with silkworms, comparison of relationship), and exhibition/habitat photography—zero quantitative user-study metrics.
Voice & tense: First-person ("we explore", "first author"); past tense for the rearing/design journey, present tense for conceptual and ethical reflections.

## 6 · Venue signal

This is a textbook DIS Full Paper (pp. 808–819) from 2023. It proves that DIS rewards honest, self-critical RtD: you do not need to claim your artifact solved an ecological crisis or improved user attitudes. Instead, DIS values unpacking tensions and complicity (e.g., care vs. control) through well-crafted artifacts and rigorous first-person/observational reflection. That aligns directly with Polyrhythm Loom’s abstract, which already promises to discuss "interpretive choices and limitations involved in translating ecological processes into computational rules."

## 7 · Take-home

Learned & new: Structuring a DIS paper into Part 1 (Material & Temporal Design Journey) + Part 2 (Critical Relational Reflection) is an elegant way to keep artifact description and theoretical critique from muddying each other.
Application: Cite Metamorphonic in Polyrhythm Loom for how it uses interactive technology to surface species-specific temporality, and borrow its reflexive honesty—when reflecting on Polyrhythm Loom’s informal exhibition encounters, highlight moments of tension or interpretive limitation rather than only smooth engagement.
Good writing sample? Yes—one of the closest genre matches to your DIS target. Its 12-page pacing, autoethnographic candor, and temporality/materiality framing are worth emulating closely.
